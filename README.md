# geospatial-panel-modeling
This project explores the compounding effects of urban heat and air pollution on informal settlements in New Delhi. We wanted to test whether the thermal blanket effect, where daytime smog traps heat and prevents nighttime cooling, disproportionately burdens vulnerable neighborhoods. To answer this, we built a high-resolution spatial panel tracking three pre-monsoon summers April, May and June, from 2022 to 2024 by combining satellite temperature and pollution data (MODIS and Sentinel-5P) with official slum coordinates from the Delhi Urban Shelter Improvement Board (DUSIB). We used a Two-Way Fixed Effects regression, and the data showed that informal settlements actually suffer an additional 0.2°C thermal penalty during high-pollution events compared to formal urban areas. Ultimately, the goal of this analysis is to provide empirical backing for hyper-localized climate adaptation policies rather than generic, city-wide mandates.
